

---- Maximum Improvment of Reconstruction-Based SSL ---

two branches
    global - reconstruction of the global image following the key augmentation of removing a large rectangular region from the image
    local - reconstruction of the actual local region based on global features




[EXPERIMENT 1] - establishing a baseline
baseline for performance of the self supervised learning-trained static global encoder

Results: SSL shows an inability to overfit to the supervised dataset when simple geometric augmentations are applied, however,
training the static encoder using augmentations does not have a discernable difference in performance

NOTE: naming errors in configs could cause some confusion

    [PROCESS 1A] - training
    SSL will be run in its current working state, 
        significant photometric augmentations
        no geometric augmentations
        "chunk" masking (as opposed to sparse)
        0.1 vallidation split 
        early stop of 50 epochs
        mse loss (as opposed to norm mse which was tried and did not work as well)


    [PROCESS 1B] - identical training to [PROCESS 1A] with geometric augmentations
    adding the horizontal and vertical flip, and 90 degree rotation augmentations


    [EXPERIMENT 1C] - performance of static encoder generated by baseline ssl
    an overfit will be performed on the supervised dataset with no augmentations

    Expected: strong overfit 
    Results: strong overfit after ~epoch 300


    [EXPEIRMENT 1D] - performance of static encoder generated by baseline ssl
    an overfit will be performed on the supervised dataset with ONLY geometric augmentations
    these will include a horizontal and veritcal flip, and 90 degree rotation, see config for details

    Expected: more time required for overfit, but equally strong
    Results: IoU still increasing after epoch 800 but nowhere near as strong as [EXPERIMENT 1C]


    [EXPERIMENT 1E] - performance of static encoder with augmentations during training
    identical to [EXPEIRMENT 1D], but uses the static encoder with augmentations during training

    Expected: no discernable difference from 1D, generalization may be improved in a way this test cannot measure, if it is 
    the same it is likely better practice to use this static encoder with the flips and things
    Results: no discernable difference



[EXPERIMENT 2] - intelligent global loss computation
Noisy gradients in ssl slow convergence and reduce quality of latent space produced

Results: weighting reconstruction 3x produces a very slighly superior overfit performance

NOTE: testing higher weights on reconstruction would be beneficial, did not test on overfit with augs

    [PROCESS 2A] - training with targeted global loss
    SSL will be trained identically to PROCESS 1B, with only the reconstructed region being evaluated in the loss function, unaltered tiles are ignored


    [PROCESS 2B] - focused global loss
    identical training scheme to [PROCESS 2A] but instead of not computing loss on unmasked tiles just weight the loss 3x on masked tiles


    [EXPERIMENT 2C] - cooresponds to [PROCESS 2A]
    overfit without augmentation is performed with the static encoder as generated by [PROCESS 2A]

    Expected: earlier convergence than [EXPERIMENT 1C] due to a stronger emphasis on true understanding of reconstruction rather than compensation for photometric augmentation in the static global encoder
    Results: later (~300 epochs behind) but equally strong overfit, slightly inferior to [EXPERIMENT 1C]


    [EXPERIMENT 2D] - cooresponds to [PROCESS 2B]
    overfit without augmentation (identical to [EXPERIMENT2C]) is performed with the static encoder as generated by [PROCESS 2B]

    Expected: stronger performance than [EXPERIMENT 2C] because other parts of image reconstruction also receive gradients, meaning more accurate photometric reversal
    Results: very interesting that a significant dip in iou happens in [EXPERIMENT 1C] and this experiment at epoch ~260, more severe in this one
    looking at tensorboard, i see very slightly more stable training with this scheme, going to proceed with it and note that testing with higher weights would be worthwhile
   


[EXPERIMENT 3] - robust local supervision
instead of a single local crop per image, lets do 4

    Results: no effect

    [PROCESS 3A] - train SSL identically to [PROCESS 1B] with a batch size of 45 instead of 60
    (this is to control for a smaller batch size due to computation requriements)

    NOTE: changed some nuance of the tile sampling too, now local selection is explicitly randomized instead of tied to the index (which was random due to pytorch shuffle anyways)


    [PROCESS 3B] - train SSL identically to [PROCESS 3A], only use 4 random local reconstructions instead of 1 for local supervision


    [EXPERIMENT 3C] - overfit identically to [EXPERIMENT 1C] with [PROCESS 3A] as static global encoder

    Expected: identical to [EXPERIMENT 1C], noisier gradients during ssl training probably dont affect anything this far downstream
    Results: slightly less stability in training compared to [EXPERIMENT 1C], likely due to lower batch size


    [EXPERIMENT 3D] - overfit identically to [EXPERIMENT 3C] with [PROCESS 3B] as static global encoder

    Expected: superior performance to all previous experiments, especially in false positives, this is because features will be strongly enforced to be local in a variety of locations
    per backprop in global encoder training
    Results: no perceptible difference compared to [EXPERIMENT 3C]
